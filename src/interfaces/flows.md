# Task flows

One screen does almost everything, so these are sequences rather than journeys between
places. The Solve screen never scrolls and never changes shape; what changes is the
state of the timer and what the notation strip is saying.

## Solve a cube, casually

The default, and the one that has to be frictionless.

Open the app. A solved cube is already on screen and scrambles itself in front of you,
which is what covers the solver warming up. No spinner, and nothing to tap.

Press a sticker and drag. That layer follows your thumb and releases into the nearest
quarter turn, and the clock started on that first turn without being asked. Drag the
background to spin the whole cube when you need a face you cannot see; that is free and
silent, because nothing about the puzzle changed.

Go wrong, tap undo, watch a turn run backwards. The move counter drops with it and the
clock does not.

The last layer falls into place and everything stops. The splits appear under the time.
Tap for the next scramble.

~~~ casual-flow
open -> scramble animates in -> [first turn] clock starts -> turns/undo/orbit
     -> solved -> clock stops, splits computed, solve recorded -> NEXT SCRAMBLE
~~~

## Solve a cube, to competition rules

Same screen, different ritual, entered from the mode chip.

The scramble is covered. Reveal it and inspection starts counting down from fifteen;
the app calls eight seconds and twelve the way a judge does. A turn during inspection is
refused rather than allowed and penalised, because applying a move there is a DNF under
the regulations and the app should not let you take one by accident.

Rest two fingers on the pads. Both fill white. Lift, and that same lift starts the clock.

Solve. If inspection ran long the penalty is already attached and the result reads the
way a score sheet does: the raw time, the penalty, the final result.

~~~ competition-flow
covered -> REVEAL -> inspecting (8s call, 12s call) -> both pads down -> lift
        -> clock starts with penalty already determined -> solve -> recorded
~~~

## Get unstuck

Tap hint. One turn appears, tagged as the solver's route rather than yours. From that
moment the solve is marked practice, visibly, and it will not touch either board — which
is why the button says so before it is tapped rather than after.

Hints follow a plan: the app solves once and hands out the moves in order, so taking one
after another finishes the cube rather than circling.

## Chase yesterday

Open the menu, tap the daily scramble. It is the same scramble every attempt today, and
today's attempts sit on their own small board, apart from the all-time ones.

## Solve something other than a solved cube

Open the menu, pick a pattern. The cube resets to solved and the target becomes the
pattern instead. Patterns keep a fewest-moves record and no time, because a pattern is
something to solve efficiently rather than race.

## Change how it looks or sounds

Menu. The palette switch is a real alternative rather than a filter, and the face
positions do not move between them, so what a cuber knows about which face is opposite
which survives the swap.

## Install it

In a browser tab the app says once that it belongs on the home screen, and why: Safari's
edge-swipe fights every drag. Dismiss it and it stays dismissed. Installed, there is no
browser chrome left to swipe and the conflict is gone.
